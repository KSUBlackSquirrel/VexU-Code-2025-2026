Today’s Tasks continued:
VEX V5 Robotics Competition Push Back is played on a 12ft x 12ft square foam mat, surrounded by a perimeter, with  two center goals in the center of the field.

In Head-to-Head Matches, two Alliances—one “red” and one “blue”—composed of two Robots each, compete in Matches consisting of a thirty (30) second Autonomous Period followed by a one minute and thirty second (1:30) Driver Controlled Period.

VURC Matches will be played 1-Team vs. 1-Team. Each Team will use two Robots in each Match.

The object of the game is to attain a higher score than the opposing Alliance by scoring Blocks in the Goals located around the field, two Long Goals and two Center Goals, and then parking in the Park Zones.

An Autonomous Win Point is awarded to any Alliance that ends the Autonomous Period with all of the following tasks completed, and that has committed no Violations during the Autonomous Period: 
At least seven Blocks of the Alliance’s color are Scored.
At least three different Goals include at least one Scored Block of the Alliance’s color.
At least three Blocks of the Alliance’s color have been removed from Loaders adjacent to the Alliance’s Alliance Station.
Neither Robot is contacting the Park Zone barrier.

Teams may also compete in Robot Skills Matches, where one Team tries to score as many points as possible.

Game rules and our interpretation of them. 
Rule as written
Our interpretation of them
<G5> Robots begin the Match in the starting volume
Robots should not exceed the volume of 18” long by 18” wide
<G7> Don’t clamp your Robot to the Field
You cannot build something that purposely anchors or attaches the robot to the field and any field element.
<G8> Only Drive Team Members, and only in the Alliance Station
During Matches, only the three drive team members are allowed at the field and no communication with other team members is allowed via communication device. Phones on airplane mode is allowed for scoring.
<G9> Hands out of the field
You may not place your hands in the field during the match or touch the field wall that causes it to touch or move game elements. The only time it’s allowed is if your robot has not moved and it’s unable to do so.and you have to do something to the robot to allow it to work.
<G10> Controllers must stay connected to the field
You must keep the V5 Controller plugged in for the duration of the Match. It can be removed after the “ all-clear” is given.
<G13>  Don’t destroy other Robots
You are allowed to defend your robot, but don't use defense in a way will destroy other robots. Purposely breaking another robot will result in being disqualified.
<G14> Offensive Robots get the “benefit of the doubt”
If damage occurs between a defensive robot and an offensive robot, the offensive robot will receive the “benefit of the doubt” since it was not playing defense.
<G15> You can’t force an opponent into a penalty
You cannot intentionally use strategy to cause an opponent to break a rule. It will not result in a violation for the opponent

Game rules and our interpretation of them. 
Rule as written
Our interpretation of them
<G16> No holding for more than a 3-count
One robot is not allow to pin trap or lift another robot and prevent it from moving for more than 3 seconds. Robots must be 2 feet apart (one foam tile) for the count to stop. Then the robot must wait 5 seconds before holding again. If a robot is held for longer than 5 seconds, the robot holding may be disqualified under the referees discrecion 
Scoring rules and our interpretation of them. 
<SC1> All scoring statuses are evaluated after the match ends. Scores are calculated 5 seconds after the match ends or once all blocks, field elements, and robots on the field come to rest, whichever comes first
All scores are calculated at the end of the match 5 seconds after the match has finished or after everything on the field has stopped moving, whichever happens first for the purpose of making sure “close calls” are discounted for scoring.
<SC2>  A block is scored if it is in contact with the inside surfaces of the plastic trough of a goal, not in contact with a robot of the same color, and not in contact with the floor
Blocks only count as scored if they are in the trough of the goal, not touching the bot of the same color, and not touching the floor
<SC3> A control zone is considered controlled by an alliance if a majority of the blocks scored in the control zone are the same color as the alliance
Control zones are only controlled by the team with the majority of their colored blocks in the control zone
<SC4>  a robot is parked if it is not contacting the floor outside its alliance colored park zone, not contacting any field elements other than the inside face of the field perimeter, the floor inside the park zone, and the plastic extrusions and the connectors that are a part of the park zone, or the robot is partially within the vertical projection of the park zone
The robot is parked if it isn’t touching the floor outside the zone, and not contacting any field elements other than the boarder of the zone, floor inside the zone, or inside perimeter wall, and partially inside the zone vertically

Scoring rules and our interpretation of them. 
Rule as written
Our interpretation of them
<SC5> Scoring of the autonomous is evaluated immediately after the autonomous period ends, points for parked robots are not included in the calculation for determining autonomous bonus, if ends in a tie both teams get a bonus of 5, any violations during autonomous period will result in the bonus being awarded to the opposing team, per rule <GG13> if both teams commit a violation then no points will be awarded.

Violations
A violation is the act of breaking a rule in the manual, there are minor, major, and match affecting violations.

Minor Violations- violations that don’t cause disqualifications
Accidental, momentary, or non match affecting violations result in a verbal warning
Warnings should serve to inform that a rule h been broken

Major Violations-  violation that results in a disqualification
Unless noted, all match affecting violations are major violation
Terrible or intentional violations may be major violations
Multiple minor violations within a match or tournament may escalate to  major violation at the head referees discretion

Match affecting- a violation that changes the winning and losing team in a match
Multiple violations within a match can accumulate to become match affecting
When evaluating, head referees will focus primary on any actions that were related to the violation.
Match affecting violations can only be determined after the match is completed and the scores are calculated.

Today’s Tasks continued:
To start creating our own gears, we needed to decide on a few values. First, we needed to decide on how far between the two gears on our drivetrain, deciding how big to make this gear. We chose a pitch diameter for this gear to be 2 inches. We also chose the number of teeth to be 36.
With these values decided, we then have to calculate the diametral pitch for our gears. Diametral pitch is the number of teeth divided by the pitch diameter, and is a very important ratio to remember. Any and all gears that will interact with this gear need to have the exact same ratio for diametral pitch.
Then, we need to form our circular pitch, where pi is divided by the diametral pitch. pi/18 = .175
We need to find the addendum and dedendum of our gear, which create the outermost and innermost sections of the gear. The addendum is the reciprocal of the diametral pitch, which is 1/18. This value is 0.0555, and is used to find the outermost point of each tooth. The dedendum is 1.25 divided by the diametral pitch, (0.0694) which makes up the inner half of each tooth on a gear. We then find the outside diameter(2.1111), by adding the pitch diameter to double the addendum. This is similar to finding the root diameter(1.86111), which is adding the pitch diameter to double the dedendum. If you were to subtract each of these values from 2, you will notice the differences are .1111 and -.1399, respectively. The sum of the absolute value of these becomes the length of each tooth.
We then take a point on our base circle and find increments of the circular pitch distance at a tangent angle from the circle, creating a spline called the involute. This involute spline is then mirrored at a line one quarter of the circular pitch distance, creating the evenly spaced distance between teeth. 

Now that all of this sketching is complete, we can extrude the root diameter to create the main portion of the gear. Then, we extrude the side of the root diameter within the involute splines, up to the addendum circle.

Scoring in High Stakes: 
Scoring in High stakes is more complicated than previous games due to the addition of the negative corners that subtract points directly from an opposing alliance’s score. With this in mind, we need to look more in-depth at how to utilize all game elements to score the highest in both regular match play and in the skills challenge. 

In regular match play, we can focus on utilizing both positive and negative corners. Skills matches are played as one team competing against other teams based on personal best scores, so all four corner modifiers are now void, now being worth 5 points each. Since there are twenty-four red rings on the field, and eleven blue rings that must be scored above any red rings, we should first fill the stakes with red rings. Each blue ring scored can be the only blue ring on that stake, according to <RSC4> and <VUSC4>. 

The highest possible score in a VURC match is 98 points, assuming zero blue rings are scored and the autonomous bonus is awarded to the red team. This is only ever possible if the blue team were to not show up to that match at all, which is unrealistic; however, it
does give us a good idea of
how scoring works for match
play.

possible score is 105 points,
utilizing every red ring on the field 
in order to score blue top rings.
